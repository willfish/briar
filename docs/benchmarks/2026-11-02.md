# Historical performance comparison: 2026-11-02

These measurements describe a **pre-integration, uncommitted Briar binary** based
on `4d135a8731dc2a571ca452cf45e48a14ef5d75a5`. They do not measure the subsequently
integrated provider catalogue or establish full TruffleHog parity. No performance
optimization was implemented for this comparison.

## Results

Median startup-inclusive wall time and median per-run peak RSS:

| Enabled Briar implementations | Briar time | TruffleHog time | Briar RSS | TruffleHog RSS | Comparison |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1, Abyssale | 252.2 ms | 885.9 ms | 14.660 MiB | 184.988 MiB | Briar 3.5 times faster |
| 612, broad selection | 16,044.9 ms | 883.2 ms | 14.617 MiB | 187.949 MiB | Briar 18.2 times slower |

Broad-selection throughput was 0.499 MiB/s for Briar and 9.058 MiB/s for
TruffleHog. Briar used approximately 13 times less peak RSS in both scenarios.
Every warmup and measured run returned the same 128 complete JSON findings on
both sides, comparing order-independent rows rather than counts alone.

## Method and provenance

- Reference: TruffleHog 3.97.2, pinned by `UPSTREAM.json`.
- Corpus: 128 files of 65,536 bytes, totalling 8 MiB of synthetic ASCII. Each
  starts with 512 `~` bytes, a newline, `abyssale `, a known synthetic 40-byte
  candidate and a newline; remaining bytes are `~`. No real credentials were used.
- One warmup and five measured runs per binary, alternating measurement order,
  warm filesystem cache, fresh empty HOME, network isolation and verification
  disabled. Both binaries received the same explicit files and detector selectors.
- TruffleHog concurrency: 16. Briar CLI: serial.
- Host: AMD Ryzen AI 7 350, 16 logical CPUs, Linux 7.3.0. This was a shared host,
  not a dedicated benchmark machine.
- The historical harness used GNU time inside the namespace for per-scanner RSS,
  excluding the supervisor. The current harness uses child-specific `wait4`,
  which also includes isolation setup. Do not treat those scopes as identical.
- Raw records remain in ignored `state/latency/2026-11-02-*.json`; this tracked
  summary preserves the conclusions and provenance, not the raw output.

## Limits

Mixed-fixture timings were excluded from speed claims. Both programs emitted
919 findings, but reference output varied in decoder labels, source lines and
one Blogger overlap-warning `VerificationError` that the fixture contract did
not accept. Equal finding counts alone were not sufficient evidence.

These results cover sparse synthetic filesystem scans, not live verification,
archives, repository history, every upstream identity or general workloads.
The historical broad-selection slowdown points to per-implementation keyword
rescanning as a profiling target, not a verified optimization result.
